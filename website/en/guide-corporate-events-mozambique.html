Why Maputo and Mozambique

Maputo is the capital and the main business centre of Mozambique. It is where ministries, regulators, most embassies, banks and the head offices of energy, telecommunications and development organisations sit. For an event that needs government officials, local media or partners from the private sector, Maputo is usually the right choice.

Mozambique matters to international organisations for its energy projects, especially natural gas in the north, its ports and corridors to neighbouring countries, and a large development and humanitarian sector. It is a Portuguese-speaking country in a mostly English-speaking region, which shapes how you plan languages and communication.

Maputo has international-standard hotels, a national conference centre and an experienced events supply chain. It also has limits: fewer large venues than Johannesburg or Nairobi, some equipment that has to be imported, and procedures that take time. Most of this guide is about planning around those limits.

Best time of year

Mozambique has two broad seasons. The exact months shift from year to year and between the south and the north.

  • Cooler, dry season, roughly April to October. Mild days, cool evenings in Maputo, little rain. This is the most comfortable period for conferences and outdoor receptions, and the busiest for venues.
  • Hot, rainy season, roughly November to March. High heat and humidity, heavy rain and a risk of tropical storms and cyclones, which hit the central and northern coast most often. Plan indoor venues, covered transfers and a weather contingency.

Dates to avoid

Check your dates against national public holidays and the period around them. The main ones include:

  • 1 January, New Year's Day
  • 3 February, Heroes' Day
  • 7 April, Mozambican Women's Day
  • 1 May, Workers' Day
  • 25 June, Independence Day
  • 7 September, Victory Day (Lusaka Accord)
  • 25 September, Armed Forces Day
  • 4 October, Peace and Reconciliation Day
  • 25 December, Family Day

When a holiday falls on a Sunday, the following Monday is usually a day off. Many decision-makers are away from mid-December to mid-January, and official calendars fill up around state occasions. [Confirm the official holiday list for the year of your event, plus any municipal holidays in Maputo]

Permits and authorisations

General guidance. An event inside a licensed hotel or conference centre usually runs under the venue's own licences. You are more likely to need specific authorisations when you:

  • use public spaces, streets or beaches, or build temporary structures outdoors
  • expect a large crowd, play amplified music late, or use fireworks
  • film or photograph in public places or use drones
  • import equipment, materials or giveaways for the event
  • invite heads of state, ministers or other officials who bring their own security requirements

Depending on the case, the bodies involved can include the municipal council, the police, civil aviation for drones and customs for imports. [Which authority issues which permit, documents required, fees and processing times: to confirm with the authorities for each event]

Build permit time into the plan from the start. A local partner can prepare the applications, follow them up in person and adjust the plan if conditions are attached.

Visas and entry for delegates

General guidance. Mozambique has changed its entry rules in recent years, including visa exemptions for short stays for citizens of a number of countries. Rules depend on nationality and purpose of travel, and they change.

  • Ask each delegate to check the current requirements for their nationality with the nearest Mozambican embassy or consulate, or the national migration service (SENAMI), as soon as dates are fixed.
  • Passports should have enough validity and blank pages. [minimum validity and number of pages to confirm]
  • Some travellers need a yellow fever vaccination certificate, depending on where they arrive from. [to confirm with health authorities]
  • For speakers or staff who will work at the event, check whether a business visa or other status is required rather than a visitor entry. [to confirm]

Send delegates a short travel note with this information, the venue address, transfer arrangements and a local contact number.

Protocol with government officials

If ministers, governors or ambassadors attend, protocol shapes the programme.

  • Send official invitations early, by letter, through the right office, and follow up by phone. Confirmation often arrives late, and the official may delegate to a deputy.
  • Agree the order of speakers and seating with the official's protocol office. Order of precedence matters.
  • Speeches by officials are usually in Portuguese. Plan interpretation and give interpreters the texts in advance where possible.
  • Official ceremonies may open with the national anthem. Check with protocol.
  • Keep the opening flexible. Arrival times can move, so build a buffer before the first session and brief the moderator.
  • Plan a separate VIP entrance, holding room and parking, and coordinate with the official's security team.

Suppliers and production

Maputo has local suppliers for staging, sound, lighting, screens, furniture, catering, transport, printing and security. Quality and capacity vary, so the choice of supplier matters as much as the venue.

  • Source locally where you can. It is faster, avoids customs and supports local businesses, which many international clients value.
  • Import only what you cannot find. Specialist equipment, branded materials or large LED installations sometimes come from South Africa or further away. Allow for customs clearance and transport. [typical customs lead times and temporary import procedures to confirm with a clearing agent]
  • See it before it is built. Ask for 3D previews of the stage, room layout or stand so decisions are made before the build, not on site.
  • Have backups. Spare projector, spare microphones, a second generator plan. Power cuts happen, so confirm the venue's generator and what it covers.

Languages and interpretation

Portuguese is the official language and the working language of government, local media and most local businesses. English is common in the energy sector, in development organisations and among senior executives. Many Mozambicans also speak national languages, such as Changana in the Maputo area or Emakhuwa in the north.

  • For a mixed audience, plan simultaneous interpretation between Portuguese and English, with booths, receivers and a technician.
  • Pan-African audiences may also need French. Other languages are possible with more lead time.
  • Translate the essentials: programme, signage, name badges, press materials and the event app or website.
  • Share presentations and speeches with interpreters before the day.

Media relations and the press

Local coverage gives your event weight with government and partners in Mozambique. The national media landscape includes public broadcasters (TVM and Rádio Moçambique), daily newspapers such as Notícias and O País, private television such as STV, and a growing number of online outlets.

  • Write press releases and media advisories in Portuguese. An English version helps international outlets.
  • Invite media with enough notice and confirm by phone the day before.
  • Plan a short press point with the main speakers, with interpretation if needed.
  • Accredit journalists, give them a space to work and a reliable connection.
  • Monitor coverage during and after the event and include it in your report.

Connectivity and live streaming

Hotel and venue Wi-Fi is fine for email but rarely enough for hundreds of delegates and a live stream at the same time.

  • Ask the venue for a dedicated wired line for production and streaming, separate from guest Wi-Fi.
  • Add a mobile data backup from a second operator. Mozambique has several mobile networks with 4G coverage in Maputo.
  • Test the full streaming chain at the venue, at the same time of day, before the event.
  • Keep a local recording so the content can be published even if the stream drops.

Payments and currency

The currency is the Mozambican metical (MZN). Hotels and some larger suppliers quote in US dollars, but most local suppliers invoice in meticais and many prefer bank transfer. Cards are widely accepted in hotels and larger restaurants, less so by small suppliers.

  • Agree early which currency the budget is in and who carries exchange-rate risk.
  • Paying many local suppliers from abroad is slow and costly. A local partner can contract them and invoice you in USD or EUR where applicable.
  • VAT (IVA) and possible withholding tax on payments to or from non-residents affect the final cost. [current rates and treatment to confirm with a tax adviser]
  • Expect deposits to secure venues and key suppliers. [typical deposit levels to confirm]

Indicative planning timeline

For a conference of 200 to 500 guests with officials, interpretation and some imported equipment. Smaller events can compress this; larger or multi-venue events need more time. These periods are indicative.

  1. 16 to 12 weeks before

    Objectives, audience and budget. Check dates against holidays and season. Shortlist and hold venues. Appoint a local partner. Send a save-the-date and first travel guidance.

  2. 12 to 8 weeks before

    Sign the venue. Draft the programme. Book interpretation, AV and catering. Start permits. Send official invitations. Open registration and accreditation.

  3. 8 to 4 weeks before

    Approve 3D previews and production design. Start customs for any imported equipment. Hotel blocks and transfers. Confirm speakers. Media plan and press list.

  4. Final 4 weeks

    Run of show. Briefings for staff, interpreters and protocol. Technical and streaming tests. Media advisory. Build, rehearsals and final checks in event week.

After the event, allow two to three weeks for the wrap report: attendance, coverage, photos and video, and budget reconciliation.

What drives cost

We do not publish prices, because they depend on the brief. These are the factors that move a budget most:

  • Number of guests and days. Catering, accreditation, transport and venue size scale with them.
  • Venue. Room hire, minimum catering spend and what is included in the hire.
  • Technical production. Stage, screens, sound and lighting, and especially LED walls and complex sets.
  • Imported equipment and materials. Freight, customs and transport.
  • Interpretation. Number of languages, booths and session hours.
  • Travel and accommodation for delegates and speakers, and the season.
  • Security and protocol when officials attend.
  • Streaming and content: cameras, editing and distribution.
  • Currency and taxes, depending on how suppliers are contracted.

Checklist

  • Dates checked against the season, public holidays and the year-end period
  • Venue confirmed in writing, with capacity in your layout, power backup and internet
  • Permits identified and applied for, with owners and dates
  • Entry requirements sent to every delegate
  • Official invitations sent and protocol agreed
  • Interpretation booked, with materials shared in advance
  • Suppliers contracted, imports cleared, 3D previews approved
  • Registration, badges and accreditation ready, including media
  • Dedicated internet line and backup tested for streaming
  • Press release in Portuguese and English, press list confirmed
  • Budget currency, deposits and taxes agreed
  • Run of show, contingency plan and contact sheet shared with the team

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time of year for a corporate event in Mozambique?

The cooler, dry season, roughly April to October, is usually the most comfortable and carries less weather risk. The hot rainy season, roughly November to March, brings heat, heavy rain and a risk of cyclones, especially on the central and northern coast. Avoid public holidays and the period from mid-December to mid-January.

How far ahead should we plan a conference in Maputo?

For a conference of several hundred guests with official guests, interpretation and imported equipment, start about four months ahead. Smaller events can be organised in less time, but popular venues and dates fill early.

Do we need permits for an event in Mozambique?

Events in hotels and conference centres usually rely on the venue's own licences, but events in public spaces, outdoor events, filming, drones and large gatherings may need authorisations from the municipality, police or other bodies. Requirements change, so confirm them for each event.

Which language are events held in?

Portuguese is the official language. English is common in business, energy and development circles. Events with government officials or local media should plan for Portuguese, with simultaneous interpretation to and from English.

Can we pay suppliers in US dollars?

The local currency is the metical (MZN). Some larger suppliers price in US dollars, but many local suppliers invoice in meticais. A local partner can contract suppliers locally and invoice you in USD or EUR where applicable.

How much does a corporate event in Maputo cost?

It depends mainly on the number of guests, the venue, catering, technical production, imported equipment, interpretation, travel and accommodation, and the season. Ask for a detailed budget based on your brief.
